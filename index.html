Foundation · why we advise the way we do

The programme's educational philosophy

Software Engineering at MFU is taught through constructivism and project-based learning: students build knowledge by doing real work, solving authentic problems, and working in teams — with ethics and professionalism formed alongside technical skill. As an advisor you carry this same philosophy into every mentoring conversation.

Pillar 01

Constructivism

Students construct knowledge through experience, not by receiving lectures alone. Advising: ask questions that let them reason to their own plan.

Pillar 02

Project-based learning

Learning is organised around authentic projects that mirror professional practice. Advising: frame course choices around the projects and capstone ahead.

Pillar 03

Active learning

Hands-on practice, systematic problem-solving, collaboration, real-world context. Advising: nudge advisees toward labs, teams, and field experience.

Pillar 04

Professional formation

Ethics, teamwork, communication and responsibility grow with technical skill. Advising: treat integrity and well-being as part of the outcome, not extras.

Graduate identity

"Adaptive, Business-Oriented Solution Architect"

Who your advisees are becoming

The software engineer's role has evolved from programmer to a professional who synthesises deep technical knowledge with business understanding and problem-solving to create measurable value. Our graduates analyse and integrate knowledge to solve diverse problems, collaborate effectively, practise with integrity, and contribute to the digital economy. Keep this end-state in view when advising on electives, field-experience track, and Senior Project scope.

The philosophy in an advising conversation

  • Learn by doing → point advisees to labs, workshops and coding practice, not just passing exams.
  • Learn through problems → connect course choices to the requirements, design and Senior Project chain.
  • Learn together → encourage team projects, peer review and seminar participation.
  • Learn in context → guide the field-experience track (Co-op / Overseas / Industry) as applied learning.
  • Reflect & improve → use each advising session to review, adjust and set the next concrete step.

Consistency note

  • This philosophy is shared across both curricula (2564 & 2569) — only the PLOs and course lists differ by cohort.
  • The authoritative source is มคอ.2 / TQF2 §1.1. If any wording here differs, the มคอ.2 prevails.
  • Communicate the same philosophy students see at orientation and on the programme web — one message, many channels.

Four core responsibilities

What you own as programme faculty

Each lists your duties, the granular evidence to keep, the AUN-QA criteria it supports, and the tools to use.

Responsibility 01

Academic Advisor

Guiding, monitoring & supporting your advisees

What you do

  • Meet each advisee at least once per semester (more for at-risk students).
  • Review study plan, GPA, credits & course selection each registration period.
  • Run early-warning: failing grades, absence, withdrawals, well-being signals.
  • Refer students needing help (financial, health, mental health) to the right unit.
  • Approve add/drop, leave & academic requests within deadlines.

Evidence to keep

  • Advising session record — date, topic, agreed action (de-identified).
  • Group/one-on-one advising attendance or screenshots (timestamped).
  • Early-warning entries — trigger + action + outcome.
  • Referral records to Counselling / Student Affairs (coded, no names).
  • Signed academic request forms — filed with the secretary.

Responsibility 02

Self-Development Plan

Your IDP, training & academic growth

What you do

  • Set an annual Individual Development Plan (IDP) aligned to your TOR and programme needs.
  • Plan training / conferences and request support through the School.
  • After training, record the impact — what you brought back into a course or research.
  • Maintain your publication and academic-position (promotion) trajectory.

Evidence to keep

  • IDP / TOR document for the year (goals + review).
  • Training certificates + post-training impact log → linked TQF3 change.
  • Publication record (journal / conference) with year & indexing.
  • Promotion-application evidence (when applicable).
  • Internal knowledge-sharing you led (Lab KM, workshop) — agenda + record.

Responsibility 03

Teaching Assessment & PLO / CLO Attainment

Showing how learning is measured & achieved

What you do

  • Map each course: CLO → PLO, and each CLO to a concrete assessment.
  • Publish rubrics with sub-criteria to students before the work begins.
  • Time assessment properly — release midterm results before the drop deadline.
  • Report attainment: use the Exit Exam and Senior Project as the main direct measures; report % attainment per PLO for the student’s curriculum.
  • Run course verification (ทวนสอบ) at end of term.

Evidence to keep

  • TQF3 with CLO→PLO mapping and teaching/assessment methods.
  • Rubric v2 (sub-criteria + level descriptors) as distributed.
  • Assessment timeline showing midterm-before-drop dates.
  • Rubric scores per student per CLO → % attainment table.
  • Exit Exam items and SP rubric mapped to the curriculum PLOs, with thresholds.
  • TQF5 + course verification; LMS grade-release & feedback records.

SOP · Seminar → Senior Project Seminar topic selection

How Seminar topics are sourced, pitched, chosen and matched — and how the topic and advisor carry forward into Senior Project 1 → 2. A supply-driven model: faculty source real topics; students express ranked preferences; the course team matches.

Course 1305392 · Seminar in Software Engineering Curriculum 2564 · Year 3 (cohorts 2566–2568) Selection unit Group (≤4) Carries into Senior Project 1 → 2
Stage 1 · Course team

Source topics

Collect from three channels: industry / MoU partners, university units, and faculty research. Every topic names a stakeholder.

Stage 2 · Course team + coordinator

Curate the topic pool

Record per topic: stakeholder, title, detail, accepted students/group, advisor, Pitching Day slot. Publish topic sheet + matching rules + deadline before Pitching Day.

Stage 3 · All students attend

Pitching Day

Each topic pitched (scope, outcome, skills needed). Pitch materials archived as evidence.

Stage 4 · Students (groups)

Group bidding

Each group (≤4) submits the bidding form: members, preferred advisor, and ≥3 ranked topics (form allows up to 4), each with an Expression of Interest.

Stage 5 · Course team · coordinator chairs

Matching

Apply the published rules: honour highest available preference; on oversubscription rank by Expression of Interest + relevant skills; respect topic capacity and advisor load.

Stage 6 · Coordinator sign-off

Announcement

Officially announce topic + advisor per group. This locks the assignment.

Stage 7 · Groups + advisors

Execution

Seminar work begins under the advisor (literature study, scoping, proposal) using the Research Proposal Template.

Stage 8 · Continuity

Senior Project 1 → 2

Topic and advisor carry forward into Senior Project 1 (plan, analysis, design, prototype) and Senior Project 2 (full build, QA, evaluation).

Student lane

What groups do

  • Form your group early (≤4) — mix your skills.
  • Attend every pitch — you can only bid on topics you understood.
  • Rank ≥3 topics; put your genuine first choice first.
  • Write a strong Expression of Interest for each (skills, prior work, motivation) — this decides ties.
  • Submit before the deadline; late/incomplete bids risk leftover topics.
  • Meet your advisor after the announcement to agree scope.

Advisor lane

What advisors do

  • Contribute topics from your research or partner needs, with clear scope and expected outcome.
  • Declare capacity — how many groups you can supervise.
  • Pitch clearly on Pitching Day: the problem, what "done" looks like, skills needed.
  • Support matching — review Expressions of Interest for groups requesting your topic.
  • Hold a kickoff with the assigned group; set the proposal timeline.
  • Shape scope so it extends naturally into Senior Project 1 → 2.
Publish before Stage 3 · coordinator to confirm Matching rules (tie-break basis), capacity per topic and max groups per advisor, the fallback when a group gets none of its choices, and the timeline (Pitching Day, bidding deadline, announcement) must be published to students before Pitching Day.
