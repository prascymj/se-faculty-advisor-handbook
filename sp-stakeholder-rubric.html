Seminar → Senior Project — stakeholder score sheet

For industry partners, topic owners and external stakeholders to assess a student team against the programme's six Programme Learning Outcomes (PLOs, curriculum 2564). Usable at each stage — Seminar, Senior Project 1, and Senior Project 2 — so growth is comparable across the year. B.Eng. Software Engineering, School of Applied Digital Technology, MFU.

How to use · where your score goes Score each PLO on a 1–5 scale (N/A if you cannot judge it at this stage) — the totals update live. When done, choose one: Submit to coordinator (sends the result to the programme's secure Google Sheet), or Copy summary / Print / PDF to return it by hand or email. Your draft stays on this device until you submit or reset; nothing else is uploaded. Handle data under PDPA — refer to the team/topic, not students' personal data. The coordinator files each result in the SP Stakeholder Assessment sheet and as evidence under AUN-QA C4 / C8.

1 · What you are assessing

Senior Project 2 Assess the delivered product: a working, quality-assured system and its evaluation — score what the team actually built and the value it creates.

2 · Score each criterion (1 = well below · 3 = meets expectation · 5 = excellent / industry-ready) — same six criteria & PLO links as the faculty Senior Project rubric

PLO 1

Ethical & Professional Conduct

Acts with integrity and professionalism — respecting data privacy, security and intellectual-property rights, and taking responsibility with regard for users and the public interest.

1 · Below — disregards ethics; unprofessional; ignores data privacy.
3 · Meets — basic ethics; professional conduct; aware of IP & data privacy.
5 · Excellent — exemplary, role-model conduct; proactively addresses ethics & privacy.
PLO 2

Problem Analysis & Requirements

employer priority

Understands the real problem in your context and defines clear, complete and feasible requirements — connecting appropriate technologies and methods to the need.

1 · Below — superficial grasp; requirements vague, incomplete or unrealistic.
3 · Meets — clear problem & scope; requirements mostly complete and feasible.
5 · Excellent — deep, insightful analysis; requirements precise, verifiable, well-structured.
PLO 3

Architectural Design

employer priority

Designs a coherent software architecture or information-system structure that meets functional and key non-functional needs, applying appropriate patterns and related disciplines.

1 · Below — design unclear or unworkable for real use.
3 · Meets — coherent design that would work in practice.
5 · Excellent — robust, scalable design justified against alternatives.
PLO 4

Teamwork & Project Management

Collaborates effectively with leadership and followership, shares the workload across a multicultural team, and manages the project with a clear, followed plan.

1 · Below — poor collaboration; unequal contribution; no clear plan.
3 · Meets — reasonable collaboration; clear plan; tasks well assigned.
5 · Excellent — seamless teamwork; actively managed plan; shared ownership.
PLO 5

Written Report & Oral Presentation

Communicates the work clearly in a well-structured report and a confident oral presentation — in English — with sound data analysis behind it.

1 · Below — poorly written/structured; unclear, unprofessional presentation.
3 · Meets — well-structured, clear report; clear, confident presentation.
5 · Excellent — publishable-quality report; engaging, persuasive, masterful delivery.
PLO 6

Implementation & Code Quality

employer priority

Builds working, maintainable software that keeps pace with current technology — with testing and sound coding standards — that could create commercial or social value in your setting.

1 · Below — largely non-functional or buggy; no standards or testing.
3 · Meets — functional, meets most requirements; reasonable standards + basic tests.
5 · Excellent — clean, optimised, professional-grade; well tested; deployable.
