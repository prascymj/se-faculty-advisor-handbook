1 · Purpose

Transparent, stakeholder-driven, continuous

Make topic selection fair and traceable — so every group works on a real, supervised topic that can mature from Seminar into a full Senior Project. Topics are sourced by faculty from real demand (industry / MoU partners, university units, faculty research), then offered to students who rank their preferences; the course team matches groups to topics and advisors.

2 · Guiding principles

Five principles

01

Real problems first

Every topic traces to a named stakeholder (industry / MoU / university unit / faculty research). No purely hypothetical topics.

02

Student voice

Groups rank at least 3 topics (form allows up to 4) with a written Expression of Interest.

03

Fair matching

Matching rules and capacity per topic are published before students submit.

04

Continuity

The chosen topic and advisor stay with the group through Senior Project 1 and 2 wherever possible.

05

Evidence-ready

Each stage produces a record usable as AUN-QA evidence (topic sheet, pitch, bids, matching, announcement).

5 · Stage-by-stage SOP

Detail for each stage

Stage 1 · Course team + stakeholders/advisors

Source topics

Who: Course team, with stakeholders and advisors.

What: collect candidate topics from three channels:

  • Industry / MoU partners — companies with an active MoU or collaboration.
  • University units — internal offices needing a system (e.g. Placement & Cooperative Education).
  • Faculty research — topics extending an advisor's ongoing research.
Output · draft list, a named stakeholder per topic
Stage 2 · Course team + coordinator (approval)

Curate the topic pool

What: for every accepted topic record the standard fields (see Topic Sheet): stakeholder, title, detail, accepted students per group, assigned/expected advisor, and Pitching Day slot.

Output · published Topic Sheet + matching rules + deadline, released before Pitching Day
Stage 3 · Course team / stakeholders present · all students attend

Pitching Day

What: each topic is pitched (scope, expected outcome, skills needed). Students take notes and ask questions.

Output · pitch materials (slides/links) archived as evidence
Stage 4 · Students, as groups

Group bidding

What: each group submits the bidding form: group name, member IDs/names (≤4), preferred advisor (if any), and ranked topic preferences (≥3, up to 4) — each with an Expression of Interest explaining fit (relevant skills, prior experience, motivation).

Output · bidding responses spreadsheet (one row per group)
Stage 5 · Course team · coordinator chairs

Matching

What: apply the published rules to assign each group to one topic and one advisor, respecting per-topic capacity and per-advisor load.

Suggested rules (confirm & publish before Stage 3):

  • Honour the group's highest-ranked available topic first.
  • On oversubscription, rank competing groups by strength of Expression of Interest + relevant skills ยืนยันเกณฑ์
  • Respect capacity per topic and max groups per advisor ยืนยัน
  • Groups matched to none of their choices are placed on remaining topics and consulted ยืนยันแผนสำรอง
Output · matching decision record (auditable)
Stage 6 · Coordinator (sign-off)

Announcement

What: officially announce topic + advisor per group. This locks the assignment.

Output · official announcement (posted + archived)
Stage 7 · Groups + advisors

Execution

What: Seminar work begins under the assigned advisor (literature study, scoping, proposal), using the Research Proposal Template as the deliverable backbone.

Output · Seminar proposal / paper
Stage 8 · Groups + advisors + coordinator

Continuity to Senior Project

What: the Seminar topic and advisor carry forward into Senior Project 1 (planning, analysis, design, prototype) and Senior Project 2 (full build, QA, evaluation). Scope may be refined but continuity is the default.

Output · SP1 proposal defense → SP2 final defense

8 & 9 · Quick guides

Your lane

Students

What groups do

  1. Form your group early (≤4) — mix your skills.
  2. Attend every pitch — you can only bid on topics you understood.
  3. Rank ≥3 topics; put your genuine first choice first.
  4. Write a strong Expression of Interest for each (skills, prior work, motivation) — this decides ties.
  5. Submit before the deadline; late/incomplete bids risk leftover topics.
  6. After the announcement, meet your advisor within [ ] days to agree scope.

Remember: the topic you get is likely the one you carry through Senior Project 1 & 2 — choose for the long game.

Advisors

What advisors do

  1. Contribute topics from your research or partner needs, with clear scope and expected outcome.
  2. Declare capacity — how many groups you can supervise ยืนยัน max
  3. Pitch clearly on Pitching Day: the problem, what "done" looks like, skills needed.
  4. Support matching — review Expressions of Interest for groups requesting your topic.
  5. Hold a kickoff with the assigned group; set the proposal timeline.
  6. Shape scope so it extends naturally into Senior Project 1 → 2.
